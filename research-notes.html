Research Notes

The working detail behind the receptor page

These are the notes. The receptor page carries the findings; this page carries the work behind them — the hypotheses that failed, the numbers, the methods, and the references. It is kept separate so the main page stays readable, and public so the reasoning can be checked.

Four Explanations, All Refuted

SR-17018 sits in the ordinary orthosteric pocket yet behaves non-competitively. If it lingers there, something should hold it. Four candidate mechanisms were tested against the deposited coordinates. All four failed.

1. Halogen bonding. The molecule carries three chlorines, each pressed against a different wall. A halogen bond is directional: the carbon, the halogen and its partner should fall within about 25° of straight. The column below reports how far off straight each contact falls, so small numbers would support the idea.

Every contact approaches side-on. These are ordinary van der Waals contacts that fill space. The same holds for SR-15099: bromine is the better halogen-bond donor, so the idea should work there if anywhere, and modelled, its contact sits 47° off straight.

2. Tighter binding. If the pocket grips this drug harder, it should bury more surface or make more contacts than the others.

It buries the least surface of the four large ligands. It is held no more tightly than fentanyl.

The amber row is not deposited data and was computed in a separate pass from the rows above it. That pass re-measures SR-17018 at 1.04 contacts per atom and 458 Å², against the 1.0 and 462 Å² printed here, which come from the native 8EFL frame. Read it as +7 Å² and two extra receptor atoms relative to SR-17018.

3. A distinct receptor shape. Fitting each structure on the extracellular half and measuring how far the intracellular ends drift: SR-17018 lands 0.50 Å from fentanyl’s, the same as morphine. Oliceridine manages 1.13 Å.

4. A second binding site. Empty adjacent volume is large in every structure examined — 1,200 to 2,000 Å³ in the extracellular vestibule — and no different for SR-17018.

The conclusion is the finding. The mechanism is probably not visible in a static structure at all. That points at kinetics, or at a receptor state that outlives the drug, and it means the next useful experiments are time-resolved rather than structural.

SR-15099 in the Pocket

No structure of SR-15099 bound to any receptor has ever been deposited. The Protein Data Bank was queried with a control to confirm the search itself worked: fentanyl returns 42 structures, SR17018 returns exactly one, SR15099 returns nothing.

Because the two compounds differ by one atom, the measured SR-17018 pose can be swapped at that position and the change measured. This is not a prediction of how SR-15099 binds. It is the narrower question of what that substitution does to the geometry with everything else held at its experimental position.

Identity is better established than this page once stated. The name does not resolve in PubChem or ChEMBL, but the structure does — CID 130431412 and CHEMBL4217435, carrying µ-opioid data (EC50 149 nM, Emax 83%) that match the published SR-15099 figures of 155 nM and 81%. CAS 2134602-44-9 sits immediately before SR-17018’s 2134602-45-0, as two compounds registered together would. No certified reference standard is published, so no laboratory can verify a sample.

Methods and Limits

Coordinates come from the RCSB PDB. Contacts are counted as unique receptor atoms within 4 Å per ligand atom, except the helix-7 count, which uses 4.5 Å and anchors each structure on conserved landmarks found by geometry rather than residue number. Buried surface is Shrake–Rupley with a 1.4 Å probe. Molecular conformers were embedded and energy-minimised from the chemical structure with the piperidine nitrogen protonated.

Three limits worth stating. These are cryo-EM structures near 3.2 Å, good enough to place a molecule and not to defend a bond length, so a real difference under about half an ångström would be invisible. None of this analysis is peer reviewed. And before 8EFL existed the receptor page carried a model of SR-17018 built onto fentanyl’s pose; it was 2.6 Å out at the centre of mass, close enough to look convincing and far enough that every conclusion about contacts was wrong.

Corrections

Claims that appeared on this site and were wrong, listed because a page about uncertainty that hid its own errors would not be worth reading.

  • That no structure of SR-17018 existed. It does — PDB 8EFL, deposited 2022. Arguments had been built on the absence.
  • That the three chlorines formed halogen bonds. The angles refute it. Plausible chemistry asserted without measuring.
  • That SR-15099 had no CAS number. It has one: 2134602-44-9.
  • That SR-15099 does not resolve in PubChem or ChEMBL. True of the name, false of the structure.
  • That SR-17018 tops out at 83% of a full agonist. That figure is SR-15099’s. SR-17018 is 75% in the assay where both were measured together.

Key Papers

The structural and receptor-level work. For the compound-level evidence — the animal studies, the overdose report and the human registry — see the SR-17018 page, which lists them in full.

  1. Medicinal chemistry Bias factor and therapeutic window correlate to predict safer opioid analgesics

    Schmid et al. · 2017 · Cell 171:1165 · PubMed

    The paper that introduced SR-17018 and its relatives, from Scripps Research. It proposed that the separation between pain relief and respiratory depression tracks with signalling bias — the idea the whole class rests on, and one later work has qualified.

  2. Receptor pharmacology G protein signaling-biased mu opioid receptor agonists that produce sustained G protein activation are noncompetitive agonists

    Stahl et al. · 2021 · PNAS 118:e2102178118 · PubMed

    The key paper for the binding-site question. SR-17018 and SR-14968 do not compete with radiolabelled antagonists and leave the receptor in a wash-resistant signalling state, while remaining reversible by antagonists in vivo.

  3. Receptor pharmacology Activation of µ receptors by SR-17018 through a distinctive mechanism

    Singleton et al. · 2024 · Neuropharmacology 258:110093 · PubMed

    Re-measured a dozen opioids with receptor numbers cut down to realistic levels, and found that much published "bias" reflects receptor reserve in overexpressing cells. SR-17018 still behaved differently from the rest.

  4. Receptor pharmacology GTP release-selective agonists prolong opioid analgesic efficacy

    Stahl et al. · 2026 · Nature 650:500 · PubMed

    The current account of what makes these compounds unusual, now that the binding site is settled: agonists can prefer receptor states that differ in how the G protein releases rather than binds its nucleotide. In mice, a release-preferring compound extended morphine and fentanyl pain relief without adding to fentanyl's respiratory effects.

  5. Animal study Comparison of the reinforcing, antinociceptive, and respiratory depressant effects of prototypical and G-protein biased µ-opioid receptor agonists

    Zamarripa et al. · 2024 · Psychopharmacology 241:2453 · PubMed

    Rats self-administered SR-17018 and SR-14968, so the compounds have abuse liability, though less than oxycodone or fentanyl. Neither produced respiratory depression at the doses tested, while the conventional opioids did.

  6. Structure Molecular recognition of morphine and fentanyl by the human µ-opioid receptor

    Zhuang et al. · 2022 · Cell 185:4361 · PDB 8EF5

    The source of both structures on this page. The group froze the human receptor with five different drugs bound and solved each one, including fentanyl (8EF5) and SR-17018 (8EFL). It is the experiment that answers where SR-17018 binds: the ordinary orthosteric pocket, not a separate site.
